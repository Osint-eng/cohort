## A. Project Title & Descriptions

### Title
**Cohort**

### One-liner (for forms / social)
AI workspace for student teams that turns briefs, chats, and notes into owned tasks, deadlines, and a fair contribution log.

### Short description (≤ 300 characters)
Group projects start as chaos: WhatsApp dumps, half-empty docs, and no one owning the work. Cohort is not a homework writer. Paste a brief or chat log — it proposes a task board with owners and due dates. You confirm. An agent nags only what is late. A contribution log records who actually did what.

### Long description (for submission form / README)
Every group project starts the same way: a WhatsApp dump, a half-empty Google Doc, and three people who each think someone else owns the slides. By week three the work is uneven, the deadline is close, and nobody can show the professor who actually contributed.

Cohort is an AI workspace built for student teams. You create a project, invite teammates, and drop in a brief, syllabus excerpt, chat export, or meeting notes. Cohort extracts tasks, proposes owners, due dates, and dependencies. You confirm or edit. The board becomes the single source of truth.

An agent watches only what is late or blocked and drafts the check-in message so the “herder” does not have to. A contribution log records who accepted, completed, and unblocked work — a record the team and the professor can trust.

Cohort does not write the assignment. It runs the project: who owns what, what is blocked, and what is due next.

It is a small multi-player SaaS, not a prompt demo. Live board, structured work items, real-time status, and a shareable view. Built with TypeScript, React, TanStack Start, Tailwind, hosted on Vercel, with managed auth and data. Models are called via API — the product is the workflow, not the model.

**Who it is for**
- Student teams on group assignments, labs, hackathons, and club work
- The person who always ends up herding everyone else
- Professors who want a clean contribution trail instead of a he-said-she-said email

## C. Demo Video Script (≈ 4 minutes)

**Target length:** 3:45–4:30  
**Tone:** Calm, direct, student-to-student. Show the product working. No hype.

### Shot list & voiceover

**0:00 – 0:25 | Cold open – the problem**  
Visual: Quick cuts of a messy WhatsApp group, a Google Doc with three cursors and almost no text, a calendar with “Group project due” highlighted.

“Every group project starts the same way. A chat full of ‘I’ll do it later.’ A doc that stays empty. And one person who ends up chasing everyone else. By the deadline, no one can prove who actually did the work.”

**0:25 – 0:40 | Title card**  
Visual: Clean title — “Cohort” + one-liner.  
VO:  
“This is Cohort. An AI workspace that turns the chaos into owners, deadlines, and a fair record.”

**0:40 – 1:10 | Create + ingest**  
Visual: Live product — create project, invite two teammates, paste a real-looking assignment brief + a short chat export.  
VO:  
“You create a project, invite your teammates, and drop in whatever you already have: the brief, a syllabus excerpt, a chat log, or meeting notes. Cohort does not need perfect input.”

**1:10 – 1:55 | AI proposes the board**  
Visual: Agent runs. Task cards appear with titles, suggested owners, due dates, and a couple of dependencies. User quickly reassigns one task and adjusts a date.  
VO:  
“It extracts tasks, suggests who should own what, and sets due dates. You stay in control — confirm, edit, or reassign. Once you accept, the board becomes the source of truth.”

**1:55 – 2:25 | Live board + multiplayer**  
Visual: Second browser window as another teammate. Status updates in real time. One task marked blocked.  
VO:  
“Everyone works from the same board. When something is blocked or late, the agent surfaces only that — and drafts the check-in message so the person who usually herds the group doesn’t have to write it.”

**2:25 – 3:00 | Contribution log**  
Visual: Open the contribution log. Show timestamps of accept, complete, and unblock events for two different teammates.  
VO:  
“Every accept, completion, and unblock is recorded. At the end of the project you have a contribution trail the team — and your professor — can actually trust. No more he-said-she-said.”

**3:00 – 3:25 | What it is not**  
Visual: Quick side-by-side: generic chatbot writing an essay vs Cohort showing a structured task board.  
VO:  
“Cohort is not a homework writer. It does not do the assignment. It runs the project: who owns what, what is blocked, and what is due next.”

**3:25 – 3:55 | Closing**  
Visual: Clean product UI on the status view + title.

## Notes for recording
- Use real (or realistic) assignment text and a short chat export.
- Prefer screen recording of the actual product over slides.
- Keep voiceover natural; slight pauses are fine.
- Show the contribution log clearly — it is a key differentiator.
- End on the live product, not a pitch slide.
